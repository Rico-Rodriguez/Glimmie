# Garden — Dig a stream

Carve little waterways through a cutaway hillside. A spring bubbles at the top, roots poke into the earth below, and your finger is the shovel. The fun is guessing where the water will go, opening the bank, and seeing whether you were right.

## A first round

A thirsty pumpkin sits below a pool. Draw a trench toward its roots. Water runs along the trench as you draw, collects behind a lump of clay, then spills through when you nick the bank. The pumpkin swells, breaks off its vine and rolls downhill. Catch it in the basket or let it splash into the pond.

## What brings you back

Next, plant a second crop on the other side of the hill. You can branch the stream, plug a channel with a thumbful of earth, or fill an upper pool and release it all at once. Carrots reveal orange tips behind the glass as their roots drink. Flowers open above ground and bring bees. The shape of the bed is a record of what you built.

## Smallest playable version

Prototype one spring, two root beds and movable soil. Start with a partly dug channel so the first short stroke releases water. Use a generous trench width, an endless spring and a one-touch soil refill. Judge the round by whether the player changes a channel on purpose after seeing where the water went.
